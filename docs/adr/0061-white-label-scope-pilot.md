# ADR-0061: White-Label Scope for the Pilot -- a Public Branded Share Link and a Stored-but-Inactive Custom Domain, Extending ADR-0048's Table Rather Than Replacing It

**Status:** Proposed
**Date:** 2026-10-21
**Epic:** E9 -- Agency Multi-Client Operations
**Governs:** US-44, FR-57 (full scope)

## Context

FR-57 names three things an agency can configure -- logo, colors, domain -- "applied to client-facing surfaces and generated reports." E7's ADR-0048 built the first two, applied to exactly one surface (the per-client report's PDF/CSV), and stated explicitly what it deliberately left out: "no domain field... no client-facing white-label surface configuration... When E9 ships, it extends this table... rather than replacing it." The stories document, having checked that nothing beyond the report reads `agency_branding_profile` at all, names this epic's genuine scope as "everything FR-57 names beyond that."

Two questions have to be resolved before this is buildable, and the PRD itself does not resolve either, as the stories document's own open questions 1 and 3 state plainly:

**What does "client-facing surface" mean, given the platform's own role model?** FR-04 names six roles -- brand owner, brand manager, brand analyst, creator, agency administrator, agency operator -- and none of them represents the agency's own end client as a distinct, logged-in Aurora user. A client company that hired Renata's agency does not have an Aurora account at all. This leaves at least two structurally different readings: (a) theme the agency's *own team's* working UI (campaign pages, dashboards) so that an operator acting on behalf of a client sees agency branding instead of Aurora's; (b) build a separate, externally shareable surface that the agency can hand to its actual client company, who has no login. These are not variations on one design -- they have almost no code in common, and choosing wrong wastes the whole epic's remaining capacity on the wrong half of FR-57.

**What does "domain" mean to actually build, for a pilot run by a small team?** FR-57 names a domain as one of three configurable things but states nothing about DNS verification, certificate provisioning, or routing. NFR-33 (cost efficiency) explicitly instructs caution about infrastructure commitments beyond what pilot-scale usage justifies, and the PRD's own framing of "Should"-priority, pilot-scoped NFRs (section 5.2's note on NFR-11/NFR-33/NFR-34 trading automation for a manual fallback) is the precedent this decision follows.

## Decision

**"Client-facing surface" means (b): a surface reachable by someone who has never had an Aurora login, not a re-skin of Renata's own team's working UI.** The reasoning: every current user of the agency's internal workspace UI already has an Aurora account, already sees Aurora's own terms of service and support channel at account level, and already knows, structurally, that they are using Aurora -- branding that UI would be interior decoration with no bearing on US-44's own stated purpose ("Aurora is my infrastructure rather than my competitor"), since nobody looking at it is being asked to believe otherwise. What *does* bear on that purpose is the one artifact Renata actually hands to an outside party: the per-client report. E7 already built that report's own document (PDF/CSV) branded; this epic adds the one thing still missing around it -- a way to deliver that report to someone with no Aurora account at all, through a page that itself carries the agency's branding, not just the attached document. This is reading (b), narrowly scoped to the one deliverable that already exists in shareable form, rather than inventing a full client portal (logins, dashboards, a persistent client identity) that no role in FR-04 supports and that NFR-33's cost caution argues against building speculatively.

Concretely: a new, public, token-gated endpoint (`GET /public/client-reports/:token`, architecture document section 5.5) renders a `client_performance_report`'s figures and download links inside a page themed with the agency's logo and colors (or the same neutral fallback ADR-0048 already established, when unconfigured) -- the first and only surface in this entire epic reachable without any Aurora credential.

**Custom domain: the field is real; the routing is not, and is recorded as deferred infrastructure, not quietly skipped.** `agency_branding_profile.custom_domain` is stored and format-validated (hostname syntax only). `domain_status` is introduced specifically to make the limitation visible rather than implicit: it can only ever be `null` or `'pending_activation'` in this pilot (enforced by a CHECK constraint, not merely a comment), and nothing in this codebase ever sets it to anything implying real activation. The actual share link served to an agency's client (section above) is always served from Aurora's own domain; the configured custom domain is surfaced back to Renata purely for her own reference when communicating with her client, labeled plainly in the frontend as pending manual activation by Aurora's own infrastructure team, not a self-service control. This was weighed against two alternatives: building no domain field at all (rejected -- FR-57 names it explicitly, and a stored-but-honest field gives Renata something real to configure and Aurora something concrete to prioritize post-pilot, consistent with NFR-33's "track the metric, do not certify it yet" posture applied to a feature instead of a cost figure); and building real DNS verification plus per-tenant routing now (rejected -- this is exactly the kind of infrastructure commitment NFR-33 asks a small team to defer until real customer volume justifies the operational cost of running it, and nothing in the stories' acceptance criteria requires the domain to actually route anywhere, only that it be "stored and validated").

## Consequences

**What Aurora gains:**
- US-44 ships with a concretely new, genuinely useful capability (a real, branded surface an agency's actual client can open without an Aurora account) rather than either a placeholder note or an unjustifiably large build. This mirrors the restraint ADR-0048 itself modeled for the report's own branding half a cycle earlier.
- The domain field gives product and sales something true to say ("yes, custom domains are on our roadmap, here's the field") without the engineering team committing to DNS/certificate infrastructure before pilot volume justifies it, which is exactly the tradeoff NFR-33's own framing endorses for cost-scaling claims and is applied here, by extension, to an infrastructure-shaped feature rather than a literal cost metric.
- The internal-UI-is-not-branded decision keeps this epic's actual build small and avoids a half-finished, inconsistent theming layer scattered across every existing agency screen (campaigns, dashboards, team management) for a benefit (interior consistency for users who already know they're using Aurora) the PRD's own role model gives no indication anyone asked for.

**What Aurora gives up:**
- If product later concludes FR-57's intent really was "an operator's own working screens should look like the agency's tool, not Aurora's," that is a materially different and larger build this decision does not attempt, and would need its own future epic -- recorded here explicitly as a reading this document rejected, with its reasoning, rather than silently narrowed without a trace.
- An agency that configures a custom domain gets a stored value and a clear expectation-setting message, not a working link under that domain. Some agencies may find this underwhelming relative to what "configure a custom domain" sounds like it promises; the frontend copy is written deliberately to avoid implying more than what is true (section 6 of the architecture document), but the gap between "configured" and "active" is a real product limitation this pilot accepts rather than hides.
- `client_report_share_link` tokens, once handed to a client company, are a bearer credential with no further authentication -- anyone who obtains the URL can view the report until it is revoked or expires. This is the same risk profile E1's own invitation links already carry (a token in a URL, no second factor), and is judged acceptable for the same reason: the data behind it is the same read-only figures the agency's own team already sees, not a write capability or a path to any other tenant's data.
